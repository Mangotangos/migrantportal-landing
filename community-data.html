Immigrants in Kansas City: quick answers

How many immigrants live in Kansas City?

About 160,492 foreign-born residents, or 7.6% of the population, live in the Kansas City area (Wyandotte, Johnson, Leavenworth, Miami, Jackson, Clay, Platte and Cass counties), according to the Census Bureau's 2020-2024 American Community Survey. That is 35% more than in 2006-2010, an increase of about 41,474 people.

Which Kansas City counties and suburbs have the most immigrants?

By share of residents, the highest are Wyandotte County, KS (17.9%), Johnson County, KS (9.2%), Jackson County, MO (6.6%). By number of people, Johnson County, KS (57,261 people) and Jackson County, MO (47,328 people) lead. Among cities and suburbs, the highest shares are Kansas City, KS (18.8%), Overland Park, KS (11.7%), Olathe, KS (11.2%), Grandview, MO (9.1%), Kansas City, MO (8.6%).

Where do Kansas City's immigrants come from?

About 47% were born in Latin America, 31% in Asia and 12% in Africa. The most common countries of birth are Mexico (45,184), India (11,769), Honduras (6,025), Guatemala (5,678), China, excluding Hong Kong and Taiwan (5,347), Vietnam (4,883).

What languages do Kansas City's immigrants speak?

Spanish is the most common language other than English, spoken at home by about 136,601 residents of the Kansas City area. About 52,893 of them speak English less than "very well". Switch to Kansas statewide above to compare.

    About this data

    What does "born outside the U.S." mean?

    People who were born in another country, including naturalized citizens, green card holders, refugees, and others. It says nothing about legal status.

    Does this page estimate undocumented residents?

    No. We publish group totals from official sources. We never estimate immigration status, never show individuals, and never map areas smaller than a county.

    What do the hatched counties on the map mean?

    A county is hatched when it has fewer than 300 foreign-born residents, or when the margin of error is at least half the estimate. Those numbers are rougher than the others, so compare them with care.

    How accurate are county numbers?

    The Census publishes 5-year estimates with a margin of error. Small counties have wider margins, and the margin is shown next to each figure when available.

    Why do other sources give different numbers for Kansas City?

    Estimates change with the area counted (the city limits, the eight counties on this page, or the wider 14-county metro), the year, and the method. This page uses Census Bureau American Community Survey 5-year estimates for eight counties, which is narrower than the official metro area, so it can read lower than figures for the full metro. Estimates from advocacy groups and models may also count people differently, so compare the area and the year before comparing the numbers.

    How often is it updated?

    Once a year, when the Census releases new 5-year data, usually in December. The date of the last update is shown at the top.

    Sources. U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey 5-year estimates: tables B05002 (place of birth and nativity), B05006 (region and country of birth) and C16001 (language at home), plus the ACS data profile DP02. Kansas City area means Wyandotte, Johnson, Leavenworth, Miami, Jackson, Clay, Platte and Cass counties, which is narrower than the official metro area. Figures from other sources, such as an earlier 135,000 foreign-born estimate for the wider metro in 2015 (Migration Policy Institute), use different boundaries and are not directly comparable.
